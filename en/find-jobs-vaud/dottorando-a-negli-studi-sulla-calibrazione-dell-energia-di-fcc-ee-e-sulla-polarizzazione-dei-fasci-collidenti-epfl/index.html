Full description

Mission

LPHE-RM and LPAP are looking for a highly motivated PhD Student in FCC-ee Energy Calibration Studies and Polariz ation on Colliding Beams .

The École polytechnique fédérale de Lausanne (EPFL) is one of Europe's most dynamic university campuses and ranks among the top 20 universities worldwide. EPFL employs 6,000 people supporting its three core missions: education, research, and innovation. The campus offers an exceptional working environment at the heart of a community of 16,000 people, including more than 10,000 students and 3,500 researchers from 120 countries.

The EPFL Laboratory of Particle Accelerator Physics (LPAP) together with the Laboratory of High Energy Physics RM (LPHE-RM) are seeking a highly motivated PhD student to contribute to beam dynamics studies and the development of advanced simulation tools for the Future Circular Collider (FCC-ee) high precision physics program, CERN's proposed next-generation electron–positron collider.

This PhD project will focus on spin dynamics, beam polarization and high-precision beam energy calibration at FCC-ee. The research will investigate and develop advanced energy measurement techniques, with particular emphasis on Resonant DePolarization (RDP) and the novel Near-Resonance Polarization Modulation (NRPM) method. The student will study spin dynamics and polarization preservation under realistic machine conditions, taking into account machine imperfections, synchrotron radiation and beam–beam interactions.

An additional research direction will explore the feasibility and potential benefits of transverse polarization in colliding beams, investigating its preservation under collision conditions and its possible applications to FCC-ee. The project will involve the development, application and benchmarking of spin-tracking and beam dynamics simulation tools, contributing to the validation and optimization of polarization and energy calibration strategies for FCC-ee. The evaluation of the uncertainties related to the center of mass energy of colliding beams is a fundamental part of these studies. An important component will be the validation of simulation models against experimental observations at existing accelerator facilities where possible.

The successful candidate will work within the international FCC-ee and CHART collaboration, interacting with researchers at EPFL, CERN and PSI. The research will contribute directly to addressing key open questions for the FCC-ee future developments.
