Full description

The mission

EPFL is part of a space robotics project that will demonstrate the capture of uncontrolled objects on the International Space Station (ISS). The servicer platform (nicknamed WILL) and a set of client target objects will be filmed in flight. For post-mission analysis and simulation, the team needs an independent, high-accuracy record of how the target and the servicer moved relative to each other. You will build the vision system that produces it.

Your role

You will own the Ground Truth Trajectory Reconstruction algorithm from design to flight data processing, and act as the technical champion of end-to-end testing on the engineering model (EM) hardware.

Key responsibilities • Propose and justify a trajectory reconstruction strategy, in consultation with CVLab leadership (monocular vs. multi-view, marker-based vs. markerless vs. hybrid) • Develop a visual odometry and pose estimation pipeline, adapted from open-source algorithms, that recovers the relative position and attitude trajectory of the target object and WILL from external viewpoint camera footage • Define the optical marker surfaces and geometry requirements for the target objects and WILL • Specify and oversee the manufacture of an optically representative target and WILL models • Verify and validate the algorithm, and quantify accuracy, robustness, and failure modes under ISS-representative conditions (lighting and occlusion)

  • Set up and lead end-to-end testing with EM hardware at the EPFL Space Center, covering the capture controller, grasper, and reconstruction chain working together

Out of scope: hardware development and control algorithm development. You will integrate with and test against the controller and grasper, not design them.

Required profile • Bachelor’s degree in computer science, computer vision, robotics, or a closely related field. A master’s degree is preferred . • Demonstrated experience in visual odometry, SLAM, structure from motion, or 6-DoF pose estimation . Experience adapting and debugging existing open-source vision codebases (e.g. ORB-SLAM, COLMAP, OpenVSLAM or similar), is an asset. • Solid working knowledge of multi-view geometry and camera calibration • Fluency in C++ and/or Python.

  • Ability to work with mechanical, GNC, and operations engineers and to explain vision results to non-specialists

• English fluency; working knowledge of French is an asset

Desirable

  • Experience with fiducial markers , optical target design, or photogrammetry
  • Experience with sensor fusion of vision and IMU data
  • Experience with data from microgravity, space, or other harsh-imaging environments , or with space-qualified processes (ECSS, test documentation, review milestones)
  • Familiarity with ROS or similar frameworks for hardware-in-the-loop testing

• Prior involvement in an ESA, NASA, or industry flight project • Hands-on experience integrating software with physical test setups (cameras, IMUs, motion platforms, or similar hardware) • A passion for the space sector!
